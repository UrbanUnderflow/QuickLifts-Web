01 / A repeatable operating cycle

Turn the runbook
into daily execution.

The agent prepares and coordinates work within its approved scope. You lead buyer conversations and commercial decisions.

01

Read the current state

Open the authorized university list and saved runbook. Review replies, stop requests, due dates, sequence state and prior commitments.

02

Choose the next work

Prioritize buyer commitments and replies, then due follow-ups, qualified new accounts and trigger-based nurture. Confirm priorities and capacity with the founder.

03

Prepare the action

Verify account fit, contact, sources, approved content and next step. Keep uncertain claims as research tasks.

04

Execute within scope

Research and draft first. Sending, meeting invitations and CRM changes require authorization covering the specific workflow.

05

Verify and record

Check the actual outcome and persisted record. Record evidence, owner, next action and due date. Reconcile uncertain sends before retries.

06

Bring back decisions

Summarize what moved, what is blocked, what needs approval and what is due next. Update the strategy only through an approved revision.

Commercial objective first: choose whether the operating goal is qualified pipeline, signed pilot value or post-pilot annual value. Track those separately. Account volume and workload should follow observed conversion and delivery capacity.
02 / Account fit and buying group

Research a real
reason to engage.

A strong account has a relevant performance or sports medicine need, a credible champion, sponsorship potential, a procurement path and a plausible pilot window.

Before outreach

  • Verify an institutional source and note when it was checked.
  • Find at least two relevant stakeholders.
  • Document one workflow hypothesis and what remains unknown.
  • Check existing records and prior contact before adding a lead.
  • Confirm priority segment, exclusions and capacity with the founder.

Map the buying group

Sports performance or strength coach; sports medicine or athletic trainer; AD or sport administrator; mental-health lead when relevant; IT, privacy, legal, procurement and finance. Include coaches and athlete representatives when appropriate.

Message structure: one audience, one relevant problem, one useful outcome and one clear next step.

03 / Outreach sequence

Every touch
has a purpose.

The runbook’s sequence is a planned progression. Replies, stop requests and delivery failures change the next action immediately.

Before Day 1

Research and qualify

Verify institutional fit, source freshness and at least two relevant stakeholders. Draft one credible workflow hypothesis.

Day 1

First email

Observation, relevant problem, useful outcome and one 10–15 minute ask. Confirm recipient and sending authorization.

Day 4

Call task

Assigned human makes the call; voicemail references the first email and stays under 25 seconds. Record the actual outcome.

Day 8

Proof follow-up

Use the existing thread and one approved workflow visual, pilot blueprint or case study. Illustrative examples remain labeled.

Day 12

Close the loop

Offer two real availability options, ask for the correct owner or request permission to reconnect.

Day 21 onward

Trigger-based nurture

Use an actual season, budget, event or new approved evidence trigger. Record the reason and next date; avoid indefinite automatic chasing.

Approved channel: Gmail. Send introductions and follow-ups through the founder-approved business Gmail inbox. Do not use PipeLists’ Brevo Send Email or email-sequence send/resume controls for this workflow. If Gmail is unavailable, block the send and notify the owner. PipeLists tracks accounts, stages, activity and next actions.
Check before every touch. Review the latest Gmail thread, replies, stop requests and prior sends. Pause generic follow-ups on a reply and stop on opt-out or delivery failure. Verify any queued sends are cancelled. Block new outreach while an existing Brevo sequence remains active; an authorized owner must pause it and verify the stop. Confirm scheduling and reply access before unattended sending.

05 / Reply handling

A response changes
the plan.

Review actual messages. Tracking events alone establish neither interest nor consent to continue.

Interested

Pause generic sequence; record the stated need and route to founder for discovery. Offer only confirmed availability with authorization.

Record / handoff: Engaged; scheduled only after agreement.

Referral / wrong person

Record referral evidence, verify the new person and suppression status; prepare a relevant introduction.

Record / handoff: Keep the account stage evidence-based; new contact is not automatically engaged.

Not now

Record the requested timing and reason. Pause the sequence and schedule an authorized revisit.

Record / handoff: Paused with restart condition and date.

Objection / existing provider

Record the objection accurately. Prepare a response grounded in approved product facts and the current workflow.

Record / handoff: Founder handles pricing, clinical, compliance and contractual questions.

No response

Continue only the approved remaining touches. After the sequence, move to trigger-based nurture.

Record / handoff: Do not advance based on delivery, opens or clicks.

Stop / unsubscribe

Stop further outreach immediately through the authorized suppression workflow; verify sequence is paused and alert the owner.

Record / handoff: Record do-not-contact status. If access fails, escalate urgently; do not send.

Bounce / delivery error

Stop the sequence; verify the address and delivery state before any retry.

Record / handoff: Report blocked delivery; never invent a replacement address.

Ambiguous send / save failure

Re-read the record and provider state. Reconcile whether the action completed before retrying.

Record / handoff: Unknown outcome stays unresolved; prevent duplicate sends or updates.

Give the work
a place on the calendar.

Choose the week

Work the queue

Friday

Inspect the evidence

Review replies, meetings held, stage aging, overdue actions, pilot health and commercial decisions. Record approved changes to the runbook.

Research, drafts, queue hygiene, verified activity records and decision briefs within authorized scope. Solutions/privacy support owns technical evidence. Customer success owns launch, scorecards and conversion handoff.

Founder / commercial owner

Account priorities, buyer discovery, pricing, claims, material commitments and policy changes. Escalations include the account, evidence, decision needed, recommendation and due date.

## Activation gate
1. Read the authorized live PipeLists University pipeline and saved runbook. Record its version/date. Reconcile differences with this snapshot; escalate material conflicts rather than silently overriding either source.
2. Confirm authenticated identity, target environment, exact list, roles and authorized actions. Do not borrow credentials, bypass owner restrictions or write directly to underlying data stores to evade the application.
3. Confirm target segment, exclusions, geography, pilot capacity, commercial objective, approved messages/assets, sender, outreach volume, working hours/timezone and budget rules with the founder. These are not set by this page.
4. Confirm reply access, suppression controls, scheduling behavior and how stopping an active sequence is verified. An email tracking event is not a human reply.
5. Begin in research-and-draft mode until explicit authorization permits further actions. Verify a small approved batch before unattended execution. No flawless execution or autonomous deployment is implied.

## Each operating cycle
- Refresh current records; review replies, stop requests, active sequence state, overdue next actions and buyer commitments before new outreach.
- Deduplicate by organization, verified contact and existing linked records. Preserve saved custom stages and user edits.
- Select accounts using approved priorities. Proposed order: buyer commitments and replies, due follow-ups, qualified new accounts, then trigger-based nurture.
- For each account, identify owner, evidence-supported stage, next action and due date. Unknown values stay unknown and produce a task.
- Prepare or perform only authorized actions. Before a send, verify recipient, scope of approval, suppression, relevant approved content, live sequence state and absence of an equivalent completed touch.
- Record actual outcome, source, time, actor and next step. Re-open or refresh the saved record to verify persistence. Do not move stage without evidence.
- On an uncertain send result, reconcile the existing attempt and provider state before retrying. On version conflict, re-read and preserve newer changes. Never retry blindly.
- End with a concise brief: completed actions, replies, meetings confirmed, changes in stage with evidence, blocked items, approvals needed and next due actions. Distinguish researched, drafted, approved, attempted, sent and delivered.

Runbook sequence: research before Day 1; email Day 1; human call Day 4; proof email Day 8; close-loop email Day 12; trigger-based nurture from Day 21.

Until then, prepare drafts and have a named owner review each touch. PipeLists Brevo sequences must remain inactive for these contacts.

Record skipped calls explicitly; a later email stage does not prove the call occurred.
